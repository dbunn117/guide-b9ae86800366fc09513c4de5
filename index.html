Start here

The first 60 seconds

  1. Check if I respond and breathe normally. Call my name and tap my shoulder. If I do not respond, call 911 on speaker.
  2. Not breathing normally or only gasping? Start CPR and use an AED if available. Follow the dispatcher. Do not delay for my phone or kit.
  3. Unresponsive but breathing normally: put me on my side, with my mouth facing down so fluid can drain. Bend my top knee to keep me steady. If a neck or back injury is suspected, follow the dispatcher before moving me.
  4. Severe low, seizure, or unsafe swallowing: no food, drink, or gel by mouth. Give rescue glucagon from my bag, following the directions on that kit and the dispatcher. Glucagon is different from insulin. Stay with me and watch my breathing.

Do not guess an insulin dose. Do not delay emergency help to check sugar, change a device, or call Jen.

If I am alert and can swallow safely, check my sugar using the steps below. If I seem low and you cannot check it, follow the LOW steps below.

Before LOW or HIGH steps

How to check my sugar

If I cannot wake, have a seizure, or cannot swallow safely, start the emergency steps above. Do not wait for a sugar reading.

1. Read my Dexcom G7 app

  1. Ask me to unlock my iPhone if I can. Open Dexcom G7. The sensor on my upper arm sends readings automatically; you do not scan my arm with the phone.
  2. Find the current glucose number in the large circle on the glucose screen, with an arrow beside it. Read the number and its units, mg/dL. Use the current reading, not an old point on the graph, a screenshot, or a past alert.
  3. Check that a number and trend arrow are both present. If readings are missing, the sensor is warming up, or the number does not match how I look or feel, use the fingerstick meter below. The arrow shows which way sugar is moving; it is not an insulin dose.

If Omnipod 5 is already open, its Home screen also shows the sensor glucose number and arrow. This is the same sensor information, not a separate blood test. Do not tap Bolus just to check sugar.

Phone locked and I cannot unlock it? Medical ID can show contacts and medical facts, but is not a live sugar reading. Use the meter in my bag. Do not spend time trying to unlock my phone.

2. Use the fingerstick meter if needed

Find the glucose meter, its matching glucose strips, and a lancet (small finger-pricking needle) in my bag. Brands vary. These are general steps; use the diagrams and instructions supplied with that meter and lancing device. Do not use ketone strips for this test.

  1. Wash and dry my hands with soap and water. Sugar on fingers can distort the result. Clean your own hands too.
  2. Put a new, unexpired glucose strip in the meter as its diagram shows. Wait until it is ready for a blood sample.
  3. Prepare a new lancet using the lancing device's directions. Prick my fingertip to get a small drop of blood. Do not use an insulin needle.
  4. Touch the strip's sample end to the blood drop as the meter's diagram shows. Wait for the result, then read the number and units on the screen. If it reports an error, follow its instructions using a fresh strip; do not guess a result.
  5. Tell the helper or dispatcher the number, units, and time. Do not enter a calibration or an insulin dose just to check sugar. Dispose of the used lancet safely in a sharps container.

If you cannot identify or safely use the meter, get help. Do not delay treating a suspected low in someone who is awake and can swallow safely.

3. Choose the next step

  • Below 70 mg/dL or the screen says LOW: go to LOW sugar. Below 54 is very low.
  • 70–180 mg/dL: my usual target range. A number in range does not rule out an emergency if I am unwell.
  • Above 180 mg/dL: above my target; do not guess insulin. At 250 or above, or if I am sick, follow HIGH sugar & ketones. A HIGH display also needs the HIGH steps and medical help.
  • Any number with severe symptoms: call 911. Tell the dispatcher what you see.

LOW sugarBelow 70 mg/dL · treat now

Low sugar can cause shaking, sweating, hunger, weakness, or confusion. Below 70 mg/dL is low. Below 54 is very low.

Use food or drink only if I am awake, alert enough to follow directions, and can swallow safely. Otherwise use the emergency steps above.

  1. Give 15 grams of fast carbohydrate. Use apple juice or glucose tablets. Read the label for the amount that gives 15 g. Juice is often about 4 fl oz (½ cup / 120 mL). Tablet and box sizes vary.
  2. Wait 15 minutes. Stay with me. Set a timer on your own phone.
  3. Check my sugar again. Use the fingerstick meter steps if available, especially if symptoms and the sensor disagree. If it is still below 70, give another 15 g and recheck in 15 minutes.
  4. Once at least 70 and improving: give a snack or meal with carbohydrate and protein, if I can swallow safely.

If I get worse, cannot swallow, or do not improve with treatment, call 911. After glucagon, I need emergency medical care even if I wake up.

Do not give insulin for the sugar used to treat a low. Avoid diet drinks. Chocolate and high-fat foods act too slowly for the first treatment.

HIGH sugar & ketonesGet help for illness or high ketones

High sugar with a lack of insulin can cause a dangerous acid buildup called DKA.

Call 911 for high ketones, trouble breathing, repeated vomiting/cannot keep fluids down, severe confusion, or worsening illness. Also seek emergency care if sugar stays at 300 mg/dL or above. Do not wait for both a high reading and a ketone result.

  1. At 250 mg/dL or above, or if I am sick, help check ketones if you know how and have the right test. Do not delay medical help to find supplies.
  2. Use the instructions for that meter and its matching strips. A blood ketone meter usually gives a number in mmol/L; urine strips use categories such as moderate or large. They are different tests. Do not assume my glucose meter can test ketones.
  3. Call Jen and seek medical advice. If I am alert and can swallow, offer water.

A stranger should not give a correction dose or inject insulin. Tell the clinician I use an insulin pump; a delivery problem may need urgent treatment. Keep my phone near me. Follow the clinician's directions.

Routine help · after immediate safety

Help with a meal bolusFriend-assisted · care-team review required

[CONFIRM WITH MY CARE TEAM]
This section needs my clinician's approval and a walkthrough with the friend who may help. It is for a meal when I am alert, can eat, and can direct the friend, or a clinician is directly guiding them. It is not an emergency treatment for an unconscious or confused person.

If I cannot safely direct you and no clinician is guiding you, call Jen or get medical help. Do not guess carbs or insulin.

  1. On my unlocked iPhone, open Omnipod 5 → Bolus.
  2. Tap Carbs, enter the actual meal's grams of carbohydrate, and tap Done. Do not enter grams in an insulin-units field. Do not enter rescue carbs for a low.
  3. With a valid reading that matches how I feel, tap Use Sensor. If unavailable or suspect, stop and get my or the clinician's help.
  4. Review the suggested dose with me or the clinician. Do not edit Total Bolus or override the calculator.
  5. Tap Confirm, review the details, then START. Check delivery progress in the app. If unsure whether it went through, do not repeat it.

Do not change settings or switch to manual mode to force a dose. Do not add injections, extra correction doses, or made-up carbs. If the sensor is inaccurate, use a fingerstick and get me or a trained clinician to follow Insulet’s instructions, including switching to Manual Mode for inaccurate sensor values. A stranger should not improvise these changes. The app must know about active insulin. If I become low, stop meal-dose steps and treat the low.

Replace the G7 sensorGuided help · use the meter during warmup

[CONFIRM WITH MY CARE TEAM]
For a helper shown how to do this, with my consent or direct clinical guidance. A new sensor is not the first step in an emergency. Use the meter while readings are unavailable.

  1. In Dexcom G7 → Connections → Sensor, choose Replace Sensor if ending early. Peel off the old patch and sensor.
  2. Wash and dry hands. Choose a new spot on the back of my upper arm, away from irritated skin, pressure, scars, and at least 3 inches from an insulin infusion/injection site. Clean with alcohol and let it dry.
  3. Unscrew the applicator cap. Do not touch inside. Hold it firmly against the skin and press the button. Lift it away. Rub around the patch three times; gently press the sensor for 10 seconds. Apply the supplied overpatch as shown in its instructions.
  4. Follow the app prompts. Scan the applicator QR code inside the Dexcom app, or enter its four-digit pairing code. Confirm pairing. Keep the applicator until both apps are connected.
  5. Standard G7 warmup is up to 30 minutes. Follow the displayed timer; no readings or alerts during warmup. If the box says G7 15 Day, its warmup is 60 minutes. Keep the phone nearby.
  6. Connect each new G7 to Omnipod 5 too: Menu → Manage Sensor → ADD NEW → TAKE PHOTO, scan the applicator QR, then CONFIRM. Manual entry needs the four-digit pairing code and 12-digit serial number. Connection can take up to 25 minutes; that is separate from G7 warmup. If prompts differ or connection fails, get help.

Replace the Omnipod podGuided help · no guessed insulin doses

[CONFIRM WITH MY CARE TEAM]
A helper needs hands-on training or direct clinical guidance for pod changes. If I cannot guide you, call Jen or my care team. Do not start a change without a replacement pod, the correct insulin, and help to finish it.

My insulin is Humalog in a 10 mL vial. Check the vial label: Omnipod 5 requires compatible U-100 insulin. Never substitute a different strength. Pods are not supplied prefilled.

Order: deactivate/remove old → fill new → pair/activate in tray → apply → insert cannula/start delivery → check → return to automated mode when prompted.

  1. In the app, use Menu → Pod → Change Pod → Deactivate; follow the prompts and remove the old pod. Tap Set Up New Pod. Never wear two active pods.
  2. Use a new, compatible Omnipod 5 pod and its supplied fill syringe. Wash hands. Follow Insulet's full filling instructions linked below, including air-bubble removal, fill amount, and the correct fill port. Keep the pod in its tray. Never fill or refill a pod on my body.
  3. After filling, place my iPhone touching the pod and tap NEXT. Let the app pair and activate it before applying it.
  4. Prepare a new site on my abdomen using the manufacturer's placement rules: at least 1 inch from the old site, 2 inches from my navel, and 3 inches from the sensor, with the devices on the same side of my body. Wash, dry, clean with alcohol, and let dry.
  5. Remove the tab and adhesive backing. Reject a damaged pod or one with its cannula already sticking out. Apply it firmly, horizontal or diagonal on my abdomen.
  6. Follow the app: START inserts the cannula; confirm secure attachment. Follow the lean-site skin-pinching instructions if applicable. Inspect the cannula, pink indicator, and site for leakage; answer the app's checks.
  7. Once the new pod is working, follow the prompt to return to Automated Mode. If the mode, sensor connection, or delivery is unclear, get clinical/device help.

There is no external tubing to fill. The pod handles cannula filling internally. If there is an alarm, leaking insulin, or suspected delivery failure, get help promptly; do not keep giving correction doses.
